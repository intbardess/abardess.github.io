Book

In 2017 I self published a science fiction novel, "Thalci Varner and the Red Children of Paradise." It follows the population of a space ship sent out to settle on a new planet when they arrive on at their destination. The journey took so long to complete that they have lost all of their history, and don't really understand the origin or purpose of their jouney. It explores themes of perspective, cultural relivitism, and how people make sense of the world around them when it does not align with their central beliefs. It is available in both (paperback and as a Kindle ebook ). I am still (slowly) working on a sequel.

Book Reviews (Blog)

I am an avid reader and enjoy watching movies and shows (mostly anime these days), and I love discussing works that had resonated with me. One of the ways that I do this is through my blog, which I started original for book reviews, but have since expanded to talking about whatever riles me up. I don't update it regularly any more, but when I finish a new book or other media that I am passionate about (whether because it is amazing or terrible), I enjoy writing up my thoughts about the work. Log of Teaching Books

At the beginning of my career as a teacher I acquired more texts than I knew what to do with, and they ended up in a stack in my classroom. Each summer, to further my learning, I would choose three or four of these books to read and record my takeaways. The result is this document, a collection of one-page reviews of all the books I read to learn and enhace my teaching pedagogy. It is written mostly for myself or another professional, to help keep track of the many techniques, philosophies, and types of knowledge that make up the field of teaching. If I ever return to teaching then I intend to keep expanding it.

Game Jam Games

Since college I have been an on and off programmer. I originally learned coding in Python, but in more recent years I have been learning to use the Godot endinge to make video games. So far the only projects I have completed were for the GMTK game jams, a competition where everyone was given 48 to build a game from scratch to fit a prompt. I did all the programming myself, but the visual and audio art assets were created in collaboration by several of my friends. These were very fun experiences, and I intend to make a more polish, full-length game at some point in my life, in addition to future game jams.
